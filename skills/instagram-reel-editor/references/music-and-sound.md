# Music and sound

Treat the soundtrack as a story arc, not a constant layer.

## Bed construction

- Prefer the user's supplied track when it is the stated reference.
- If a short opening is explicitly approved, repeat it only for that intro window. Then crossfade into the natural continuation of the source track; do not loop the same phrase for the full Reel.
- Use a short overlap/crossfade at any source handoff. Audition the handoff rather than trusting a waveform that merely touches.
- Preserve a clean ending. Do not let an automatic final pickup make the music louder than the dialogue or previous section.

## Volume arc

Write an explicit automation lane in composition time. Typical starting shape: full opening, gradual fade after the opening argument, quieter middle while speech carries the story, and a restrained rise for the final CTA. Adjust against the actual voice level; a single scalar volume is not a mix plan.

## Dialogue and effects

- Dialogue remains the primary signal. Use dynamic carve/ducking when music overlaps speech.
- Mute external B-roll audio unless its natural sound is part of the brief.
- Add SFX only when they clarify a meaningful beat or transition. Remove a sound effect when the visual reset already communicates the change.
- Verify the intro handoff, a quiet middle section, and the ending by listening to rendered audio—not only by inspecting metadata.
